# H12 take-profit books versus the base trial

Paper only. Read from the 2026-10-14 07:32 UTC books and from `reports/daily/2026-10-14.md`. No strategy file, hash, gate, or book was changed. No signed transaction.

The take-profit books are behind the base trial by about $150 to $180. That gap is not the take-profit exits. On trades that share a pool and an entry time, the take-profit book is a few tens of dollars ahead, the 18% stops are the same trades, and selling back to SOL adds about $2 of slippage or less. The extra loss is 47 shared pools the take-profit books entered later, mostly the next day. Those are different trades. The earlier base entry collected more crossed-bin fees. The base book also holds 15 pools the take-profit books never opened, and those pools lose about $406, which hides part of the later-entry gap.

## Inferred

- Exit slippage walks current pool depth. The published net uses the tournament scan's pool at mark time. This note does not re-fetch pools. It splits that published net into slippage and rent with the cost formula. It does not claim the walk equals a Jupiter quote.
- Fees between marks are the pool's fee rate at the mark, times hours since `lastFeeMarkAt`. They are not a swap tape. See the recorder gap below.
- Replacing crossed-bin fees with the stored active-bin estimate, and leaving inventory and rent as they are, is an accounting sketch. It is not a second simulation. Fee tokens also change the exit walk by a small amount, and that piece is not re-priced here.
- Same-pool, different-entry pairs are not a controlled test of the take-profit. Entry price, hours open, and the path differ. They explain the book totals. They do not measure upside the take-profit cut off after a shared entry.

## (b) Extra swap and rent from take-profit exits

On the same-entry take-profit exits, extra slippage versus the base is **$2.30** (tp10), **$1.26** (tp15), and **−$0.17** (tp25 and tp40). Tx plus sunk rent is **$7.67 lower** on tp10 and tp15, which is one bin array the still-open base still covers, and **equal** on tp25 and tp40.

Across all 34 same-entry positions, the slippage gap is $2.43, $1.34, $1.62, and −$1.88. Rent does not explain the book gap.

The open marks already include a full liquidation estimate. A take-profit close does not add a second cost on top of that estimate. Where the take-profit book pays less slippage, the usual reason is that it left a thin pool before the base's still-open inventory was marked. pill-SOL, a different-entry take-profit, is the large case: take-profit slippage about $7, base still-open slippage $17.51.

## (d) Are the losers the same trades?

On the 34 same-entry pairs, yes.

- All 34 have the same sign of net. 27 are losses in both books.
- Four are stop-loss in both. On tp10 and tp15 the two nets match to the cent (−$253.44 combined). On tp25 and tp40 the stop gap is about $0.40.
- The only reason mismatch on a same-entry pair is take-profit versus still open (4, 4, 2, and 2). None of those base positions has stopped.
- The extra stop in the take-profit books (19 versus 18) is in the later-entry set.

The books do not share one loser list once entry time is allowed to differ. On those 47, ten stops hit both, five hit only the take-profit book, one base stop is still open in the take-profit book, and one base stop was a take-profit in tp10 (PURR-SOL).

## Recorder gap

The Helius recorder was down from about 2026-10-13T17:08Z until 2026-10-14T07:28Z. This comparison uses the same 07:32Z marks as the daily. Anything still open, and anything closed by this morning's mark, accrued fees from the Meteora API fee rate times elapsed hours, not from websocket swaps. A stop or take-profit that printed inside that window and then recovered is not in these books.

SANTA-SOL's take-profit closed at 2026-10-13 07:23 UTC, before the gap. Its base twin stayed open through the gap, so that pair's "forgone" includes unverified base fees. COCKROACH-SOL, PUMPCAT-SOL, and SI-USDC closed on the 07:32Z mark, so both sides of those pairs include the gap. The crossed-bin versus active-bin spread on the base book is $2,052 versus $304. The later-entry fee gap that drives the result is the crossed-bin number, and the missing tape sits inside the last day of it.

## Verdict

The take-profit sub-books lost more than the base trial because they entered 47 of the same pools later and, under the crossed-bin fee model, earned about $490 to $530 less in fees on those entries, then wore a worse inventory mark and a few extra 18% stops. Same-entry take-profit exits did not give away a later gain worth more than a few dollars, and they did not pay a meaningful extra swap to SOL. The 15 pools the base holds alone are net losers, so the headline gap is much smaller than the later-entry gap. No 96-hour out-of-range timeout has fired. The 14-hour recorder hole means the last day's crossed-bin fees, which are the fees this conclusion leans on, were not observed.
